## Summary

A New York jeweler bets on gemstones and basketball to escape his debts.

## Moment

Kevin Garnett walks into Howard Ratner's jewelry store. Amid the chaos of having a celebrity in the shop, Howard receives a box of fish. Hidden inside is an Ethiopian opal, pristine and uncut. Excited, Howard shows the stone to Garnett, prefacing it with an entrancing story about Black Jews in Ethiopia slaving away in mines. The story and stone capture Garnett's imagination, and he expresses that he must have the stone.

The scene shows Howard's ability to craft a compelling story around the jewel and convince Garnett of its authenticity. As a master storyteller, Howard can paint a rose gold film over any piece of jewelry in his shop. But this ability to spin a story works both for and against Howard. Often, he places the same rose gold film on his own ideas. He sells himself on both good and bad bets as well as he sells others on good and bad jewelry.

## Lesson

How you deceive others is how you deceive yourself.

## Question

Why didn't Howard return Arno's portion of the money and make his crazy bet on the remaining fifty grand? His payoff would have still been several hundred thousand dollars, which, while not a million, would have allowed him to pay off his debts.
