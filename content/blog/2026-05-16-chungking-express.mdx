## Summary

Two police officers navigate romance and rejection in Hong Kong's Chungking Mansion.

## Moment

One scene that stuck with me was the meeting between the second police officer and his former girlfriend in the convenience store. She runs into him by coincidence while on a date with another man and, after making small talk, gallivants off with her new lover and leaves him to pay for her snacks. The officer is left slightly bewildered and extremely melancholy after the chance encounter. She even tells him that she "throws away" men when they no longer interest or suit her. Her remarks reveal how empty the officer's relationship with the flight attendant was, despite his genuine feelings.

As he exits the convenience store after the humiliation of paying for her snacks and drinks, the officer must choose whether to reject romance. Up to this point, pursuing love has only hurt his heart and dignity. Does he open Faye's letter and welcome another chance at embarrassment? He tosses the letter in a trash box and leaves. Rain pours, signaling defeat.

But he returns. The audience sees his hand fish through the box for the letter, now drenched. He delicately opens the wet paper and uses the warming rack in the convenience store to dry off the note, revealing a boarding pass for one year in the future. The night, which started in humiliation, ends in hope.

## Lesson

The path to romance requires accepting rejection.

## Question

What happened to the first police officer? His story ends without resolution after the mysterious woman in sunglasses wishes him happy birthday. There isn't enough to suggest that their relationship continues, but maybe this is a commentary that his love story has not found catharsis yet.
