## Summary

After an attempt at her life and four years in a coma, an assassin embarks on a revenge quest to kill her former associates.

## Moment

I enjoyed the scene in Okinawa when the Bride meets Hattori Hanzō, a retired swordsmith. Initially, she enters Hanzō's sushi shop as a tourist, with the chef enthusiastically praising her minimal Japanese. The scene feels out of place in the film, as if the audience has stepped out of the plot and into real life. After correcting her pronunciation and serving her nigiri, he finally asks why she is in Okinawa. She has come for his renowned swords.

Once she reveals her true intentions, the scene shifts from jovial to serious. They switch to Japanese, the Bride suddenly fluent. The two cast aside their cultivated masks: no longer sushi chef and tourist, but swordsmith and swordseeker.

## Lesson

There is honor in owning one's actions regardless of the cost.

## Question

Why did Tarantino censor the name of the Bride? It seems unnecessary, though it may become clear in the second movie.
